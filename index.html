Trace Studio

The "Why"

Trace is a soft robotic telepresence system that recreates physical touch across distance.

Instead of relying on rigid humanoid robotics or wearable haptics, Trace uses a tendon-driven compliant arm embedded within a plush form to translate human motion into real-time physical interaction.

Our goal is to make long-distance communication feel physically present by combining continuum robotics, computer vision, and low-latency motion control in a compact, consumer-friendly form.

Current Progress

We have developed and tested multiple versions of the mechanical architecture, including a tendon-driven continuum arm built around a pultruded fiberglass spine for improved stiffness and stability.

The actuation system has transitioned from hobby servos to NEMA 17 stepper motors with TMC2209 drivers and custom 6:1 planetary gearboxes. This allows the arm to move more smoothly and precisely.

The current prototype is powered by an ESP32 control system and uses MediaPipe for real-time human pose estimation. We are now testing the complete motion pipeline from vision tracking to physical movement.

How It Works

Vision Tracking

MediaPipe detects the movement and position of the user through a camera.

Tendon-Driven Mechanism

Stepper motors pull tendons that bend and control the compliant robotic arm.

Smooth Movement

Custom gearboxes and motor drivers create controlled, precise, and fluid movement.

Soft Exterior

The mechanism will be placed inside a soft exterior designed to feel comfortable and safe to hold.

Current Focus

Our immediate focus is refining tendon tensioning, validating the new stepper-driven actuation system, and designing the housing and soft outer shell.

We are also working on tendon routing, slack compensation, compliant joint optimization, gearbox design, and additive manufacturing for functional mechanical assemblies.

A Note on Distance

Hmm. Where do I start?

I used to think the hardest part of a long-distance relationship was missing the physical intimacy and the moments when you simply wanted to be beside someone.

After a year of flying back and forth, fighting schedules, and trying to make a relationship work through a screen, I started to realize it was something broader.

It was presence.

Being there when something happened during their day. Sitting next to someone without needing a reason to call. Sharing the small, ordinary moments that never make it into a FaceTime conversation.

We have become incredibly good at sending someone's voice, face, and words across the world. Somehow, the simplest physical parts of being together are still left behind.

I started building Trace because I could not stop thinking about that gap. I wanted to create something that could make “I'm here for you” feel like more than words.

I do not know yet if I have figured out how to do that.

But I am trying.
